# firstmargin

### A writing community where reader demand becomes writer opportunity.

firstmargin connects the two sides of writing that most platforms keep apart. Readers can say what they want to understand. Writers can see that demand, respond with useful work, and earn an audience through relevance and quality rather than arriving with a large following.

It combines thoughtful discovery, open publishing, reader questions, writing competitions, collections, and community participation in one responsive web experience. Primary articles remain free to read.

## Why firstmargin exists

Writers often struggle with two questions: what should I write, and how will anyone find it? Readers have the opposite problem. They know what they want to learn, but search and social feeds do not always lead them to the right explanation.

firstmargin closes that gap.

Readers ask questions and support existing requests. Writers receive a ranked opportunity feed based on subject fit, demand, and freshness. A response can stay concise inside the discussion or become a full published article. Everyone who followed the question can return when an answer appears.

## What makes it different

### Reader demand guides writers

Questions are a first-class community space, not comments attached to an article. Similar questions can be merged into shared demand instead of splitting attention across duplicates. Writers can claim opportunities, decline irrelevant requests, or publish a response linked back to the original need.

### Discovery is not a popularity contest

firstmargin combines topic interest, followed writers, freshness, reading quality, and controlled exploration. It deliberately limits repeated authors and topics so new writers have a real path into the feed. Readers can also see why a story appeared and mark recommendations as irrelevant.

### Published knowledge stays open

Primary articles are public. Membership pays for extras such as early releases, workshops, summaries, audio tools, creator updates, and direct requests. The free reading experience does not become worse to make the paid tier look useful.

### Competitions create another route to recognition

Writers can enter themed, timed, collaborative, and reader-choice competitions. Contests support reader voting, judge scoring, results, disqualification, appeals, and audit records. Winners receive bounded promotion rather than a permanent advantage in recommendations.

### Short and deep reading can coexist

Writers can publish long-form articles or focused short reads. Separate story and short series offer ordered reading paths; depth links help a reader move from a quick explanation to a fuller treatment.

## Who it is for

### Readers

Readers discover articles and short reads, follow writers, save stories, create collections, comment, ask questions, vote, report harmful content, and shape future recommendations through meaningful reading behavior.

### Writers

Writers use the same account. There is no application process or progressive role system. Any signed-in member can draft, publish, respond to questions, build a public profile, enter competitions, create story or short series, host eligible member experiences, and view writing analytics.

### Community participants

Reading and publishing are not separate identities in firstmargin. A reader can answer a question, a writer can judge a competition, and either can curate a collection or join a discussion. The product is built around participation rather than follower counts alone.

### Members

Membership adds optional experiences while public articles remain readable. Members can receive eligible early access, summaries, workshops, creator extras, and direct-request benefits.

### Moderators and administrators

Staff review reports and competition activity. Moderation decisions are designed to be scoped, reversible, appealable, and auditable. The policy is approved, but destructive enforcement is not active yet. Current staff controls record reviews and recommendations without silently removing content or accounts.

## Core experiences

### Discover and read

- Editorial and personalized home feeds
- Latest, popular, and recent discovery modes
- Search across articles, writers, short reads, and reader questions
- Recommendation explanations and reader controls
- Reading progress, history, saves, comments, follows, and reports
- Article summaries and browser read-aloud support

### Ask and respond

- Independent question feed and question detail discussions
- Duplicate-aware question creation
- Question and answer voting
- Question follows and participant notifications
- Writer opportunity ranking by topic fit and demand
- Short answers or linked published responses
- Direct requests to eligible writers

### Write and publish

- Persistent drafts and autosave
- Conflict-aware editing across stale sessions
- Structured article blocks and cover images
- Preview, immediate publication, scheduling, editing, and unpublishing
- Immutable published revisions
- Long-form articles, short reads, and separate ordered story or short series
- Writer profiles and performance analytics

### Participate and organize

- Writing competitions with entries, voting, judging, results, and appeals
- Public and private collections with ordered reading lists
- Personal saved library and reading history
- Notifications for questions, writers, competitions, workshops, and membership activity
- Workshops, creator updates, and early access

## The firstmargin loop

1. A reader discovers a useful story or asks for one that does not exist.
2. Community demand makes the request more visible.
3. A relevant writer finds the opportunity and responds.
4. The response enters discovery without depending only on follower count.
5. Reading depth, saves, follows, reports, and discussion improve future discovery.
6. The reader returns for answers, writers, collections, and competitions they chose to follow.

## Product principles

- Reader demand should produce useful writing opportunities.
- Primary knowledge stays accessible without an article paywall.
- Distribution should reward relevance and reader value, not only audience size.
- Recommendations should be explainable and controllable.
- Writers keep ownership of their voice and formatting.
- Private reading behavior is not sold as an advertising product.
- Trust, accessibility, responsive design, and failure recovery are part of the product.

## Current state

The main reading, publishing, discovery, question, collection, short-read, membership, notification, competition, and staff-review flows are connected to persistent backend data. The application also has automated contract, database integration, browser, accessibility, and SEO checks.

Work still remains before a public production launch. Live payment, Google, email, AI, monitoring, and production-domain checks need deployment credentials and final verification. Moderation enforcement also needs its complete expiry, appeal, notification, and audit workflow before activation.
